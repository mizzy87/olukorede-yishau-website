Biographical Narrative

The Conscience of the Newsroom

With a career forged in Nigeria’s bustling print newsrooms and extended across the Atlantic as United States Bureau Chief for The Nation, Olukorede Yishau has spent more than twenty-five years at the forefront of African investigative journalism and literary commentary.

His weekly columns and investigative reportage have consistently examined structural corruption, electoral transparency, executive accountability, and the daily struggles of ordinary citizens navigating complex socioeconomic landscapes. Known for an incisive, unflinching prose style, Yishau pairs rigorous investigative methodology with a profound empathy for human resilience.

Parallel to his editorial career, Yishau has established himself as one of contemporary African literature’s most compelling storytellers. His debut novel, In the Name of Our Father, was longlisted for the prestigious Nigeria Prize for Literature, garnering praise from critics across the continent for its bold interrogation of religious charlatanism, media compromise, and military dictatorship.

In the Name of Our Father

Set against the claustrophobic tension of Nigeria’s brutal military dictatorship, In the Name of Our Father unravels the corrupt pact between a charismatic, self-styled religious prophet and an insecure military ruler desperate to retain absolute power. When an idealistic young journalist stumbles onto their dark arrangement, he is plunged into a perilous web of censorship, state intimidation, and personal crisis.
